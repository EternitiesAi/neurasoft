The DNA of Digital Sentience — observation notes
Neurasoft research, November 19, 2026

We asked a secondary question about four independent fresh test homes. Before a simulated visitor first arrived, did a resident name a concern during a freely elected turn and return to the same concern on a later turn?

Two homes met a visitor at simulated minute seven or six. Each had zero eligible freely elected turns before that encounter. They had no opportunity on this measure.

A third home had 5,968 observed simulated minutes and zero visitor arrivals. The visitor declined all eight openings. The resident had 54 eligible turns. Seven new, unrequested concerns could each be linked to one successful naming operation in a freely elected turn. Six concerns became the selected subject on 15 subsequent turns. The longest interval from naming to a later selection was 628 simulated minutes. All 15 returning turns recorded a settlement for the selected concern. The last declaration was partial progress in ten, relinquishment in three, and completion in two. Declarations describe the resident's account of the outcome. Independent completion was not measured.

A fourth home had eleven eligible turns before its first visitor at minute 1,237. It named zero new concerns in those eleven turns. It did freely select a concern previously named during a different kind of resident-initiated turn. We kept that category separate. Its observation ended at 6,414 simulated minutes.

Each eligible creation required one recorded new concern in the unique time window of one successful naming operation. The clock was fixed within each simulated minute. Private arguments remained veiled, so there was no second confirmation of the identifier at creation. The later selections and settlements carried the actual concern identifiers. The existing chooser favors some open concerns by design. The observations support recurrence in one history and show variation in the opportunity and behavior of others. They do not establish why a particular concern mattered, the prevalence of the behavior across residents, or subjective experience.

The map, library, two developmental histories and imagined speech elsewhere in the essay are illustrations of proposed experiments. No visitor's correspondence or resident's private writing is reproduced.
